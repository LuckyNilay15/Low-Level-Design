1  S-> Single responsibilty principle.
 A classs should have one and only one reason to change. 
 It shiuld have one job/responsibilty.

If not---> Tight coupling, low cohesion, hard to maintain, hard to test, hard to debug, hard to extend.



2. O-> Open/Closed Principle(OCP):

  class/module/function should be open for extension but closed for modification.
  Interface: Abstract class with pure virtual methods.
Interface + Inheritance = OCP

3.L-> Liskov Substitution Principle(LSP):

derived class should be sustitutable for their base/ parent class.
should only extend the behaviour of bvase class not replace in a way that voilates the parent's contract.

4. I-> Interface Segregation Principle(ISP):

client --> interface use (not use)--> should not implement.

break into various small interfaces.

5. D-> Dependency Inversion Principle(DIP):

High level module should not depend on low level module.
Both should depend on abstraction.
Abstraction should not depend on details. Details should depend on abstraction.

Abstractions should not depend upon details."
What it means: The Interface shouldn't be molded or designed just to fit one specific piece of hardware or software.

Analogy: Imagine a wall socket (the abstraction). The wall socket is a standard shape (two or three simple holes). The socket does not change its shape depending on whether you are plugging in a TV, a laptop, or a fridge. The socket is independent. If the wall socket changed its shape specifically to fit your TV's weird wire, that would be an "abstraction depending on a detail."

In Code: Your 
IDatabase
 interface shouldn't have a method like saveToSQLTables(). If you do that, your abstraction is now depending on a "SQL" detail. Instead, it should just be 
save()
. The abstraction remains pure and completely ignorant of how the saving actually happens.

2. "Details should depend upon abstractions."
What it means: The low-level implementation must conform to the rules set by the Interface.

Analogy: Since the wall socket (abstraction) defines a strict 3-prong standard, LG, Samsung, and Sony (the details) must manufacture their TV plugs to match that exact 3-prong standard. The TVs depend on the socket's design.
